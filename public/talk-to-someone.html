What TalkLive can and cannot be

Honesty first: these are ordinary people, not counsellors. That is often exactly what helps.

Another person, not a bot

Every match is a real adult who pressed the same button you did, usually because they also wanted to talk.

No one you know

You can say what you cannot say to friends or family, because this person is outside your life entirely.

Text if speaking is hard

If you cannot talk out loud - a shared room, a late hour, a tight throat - Tap to Chat lets you type instead.

Not the right person? Next

Not every match will be a good listener. You can move on at any moment without explaining.

Keep the people who helped

If someone was kind, you can both add each other as friends and talk again another day.

Not a crisis service

Strangers are not trained to help in an emergency. For urgent help, use a crisis line - listed below.

How to get the most from talking to someone new

A few small things make it much more likely that you leave the conversation feeling better.

Pick voice or text

Voice feels closer; text is easier when you are tired, upset or not alone.

Say what you want

"I have had a rough day and just want to chat" tells the other person how to help.

Keep your details private

You can share feelings without sharing your name, school, workplace or location.

Leave when it stops helping

If a conversation drains you, tap Next or close the tab. You are allowed to.

If it is urgent, please start here

If you are thinking about suicide or self-harm, or you are in danger, please contact a trained crisis service now rather than a random chat. In the United States you can call or text 988 (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline). In the UK and Ireland, Samaritans answer on 116 123, free, any time. In India, the government's Tele-MANAS line is 14416. Anywhere else, findahelpline.com lists free, confidential helplines by country. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

Why talking to a stranger can help

Saying something out loud changes it. A worry that loops in your head for hours often shrinks once it is put into sentences, and a stranger is the easiest person to say it to: they do not know your family, they will not bring it up next week, and they have no stake in what you decide.

Loneliness is also not the same as being alone. You can be surrounded by people and still feel that nobody is really listening. The TalkLive Journal looks at what actually helps with loneliness, and one of the consistent findings is that small, warm interactions with people outside your circle matter more than they seem to.

How to ask for the kind of conversation you need

The person you are matched with cannot read your mind, and most people default to small talk. If you want to vent, say so: "Can I just tell you about my day? I do not need advice." If you want distraction, say that instead: "I am stressed - tell me something good about where you live." Most people are glad to be given a clear role.

If you would rather listen than talk, that works too. Plenty of people on TalkLive want someone to hear them out, and being that person for a few minutes can be its own relief. Listening is a skill, and it is one worth practising.

Protecting yourself when you are feeling low

When you are upset, it is easier to overshare and easier to be taken advantage of. Keep your name, address, workplace, social media and photos to yourself. Be wary of anyone who becomes intensely close very fast, asks to move to another app straight away, or brings up money. Kind people do not need any of those things to keep talking to you.

If someone is cruel, end it. Tap Report or Block - it takes a second, the other person is not told why, and you will not be matched with them again. Then, if you still want to talk, try someone else.

Frequently asked questions

Is there someone I can talk to right now for free?

Yes. TalkLive matches you with another adult for a free voice call or text chat, usually within seconds at busy times. No account is needed.

Are the people on TalkLive counsellors or therapists?

No. They are ordinary adults using the same app as you. For mental-health support or a crisis, use a trained helpline such as 988 in the US, Samaritans on 116 123 in the UK and Ireland, or findahelpline.com elsewhere.

Can I talk to someone anonymously?

Yes. You do not need to give a name, email or phone number, and there is no video. The other person only knows what you choose to tell them.

What if I do not want to talk out loud?

Use Tap to Chat. It starts a text conversation and never asks for microphone permission.

What time is it easiest to find someone?

TalkLive is busiest between roughly 15:00 and 21:00 UTC, but people are online around the clock. If a search takes a while, it is worth waiting a minute or trying text chat.
