An honest note on city matching

Worth reading before you go looking for a city filter that does not exist.

Country-level filtering

You filter to a country; cities arrive in proportion to their share of that country's traffic. No chat service can reliably do better than that.

Which is the point

City-level targeting of strangers would be a safety problem, not a feature. Anything offering it is guessing from an IP address.

Just ask

"Where in the country are you?" is the single most reliable opener on the platform. Ten seconds and you know.

Big cities dominate

Traffic concentrates hard in the largest few cities, so filtering to a country largely means matching its metros.

Cities are multilingual

Dubai, Toronto, London and Kuala Lumpur match across four or five first languages each.

Timezone is per country

Every city page lists its country's peak, which is when that city is actually online.

India

Mumbai — the longest commutes in the country, which is exactly when a voice call beats typing.
Delhi — a mix of Hindi, Punjabi and English in a single conversation is completely normal here.
Bangalore — a tech workforce that keeps US hours, so its late-night users overlap with American afternoons.
Hyderabad — Telugu and Urdu speakers in the same city, often in the same chat.
Chennai — Tamil-first, with English used freely for anything technical.
Kolkata — Bengali conversation culture — long, unhurried, argumentative in the friendliest way.
Hindi, English, Bengali and Tamil · UTC+5:30 (IST) · busiest 9pm – 2am IST · all of India

Pakistan

Karachi — the country's largest city and the most linguistically mixed room you will land in.
Lahore — Punjabi and Urdu switch mid-sentence and nobody comments on it.
Islamabad — a student-heavy population that skews toward English practice.
Faisalabad — a working city where late-night is genuinely late — 1am is not unusual.
Urdu, Punjabi, English and Pashto · UTC+5 (PKT) · busiest 9pm – 3am PKT · all of Pakistan

United States

New York — the only US city where 2am chat traffic is as heavy as 10pm.
Los Angeles — three hours behind the east coast, so it keeps the country online after midnight ET.
Chicago — central time makes it the bridge between the two coastal peaks.
Houston — genuinely bilingual — English and Spanish matches are equally likely.
Miami — the busiest Spanish-language chat city in the country.
English and Spanish · UTC−5 to −10 (ET through HT) · busiest 8pm – 1am local, in four waves · all of the United States

United Kingdom

London — more languages spoken per square mile than anywhere else on this list.
Manchester — a student city, so term time and holidays visibly change who is online.
Birmingham — large South Asian communities mean Urdu and Punjabi matches are common.
Glasgow — the accent is the single most commented-on thing in any UK match.
English · UTC+0 / UTC+1 (GMT/BST) · busiest 8pm – midnight GMT · all of the United Kingdom

Frequently asked questions

Can I chat with people in my own city?

Only indirectly — filter to your country and your own city will be well represented, since matching is proportional to where traffic comes from. There is no city-level filter, deliberately.

Why is there no city filter?

Two reasons. Technically, city detection means IP geolocation, which is wrong often enough to be useless. More importantly, letting strangers target each other by city is a safety problem rather than a feature.

How do I find out what city someone is in?

Ask. It is the most common opening question on the platform and almost nobody minds answering at city level. Share only what you are comfortable sharing in return.

Which cities have the most users?

Consistently the largest metros in the highest-traffic countries — Mumbai, Delhi, Karachi, Lagos, Jakarta, São Paulo, Manila, Cairo, Istanbul and the big US cities.
