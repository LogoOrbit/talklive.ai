How to run a language exchange that works

Say in the first sentence that you are learning and offer the swap explicitly - half in their language, half in yours. That framing turns you from someone using them into someone trading with them, and acceptance rates go up sharply.

Then agree on correction. "Correct me when it matters, ignore small things" is the setting most people want, and saying it out loud prevents both failure modes: being corrected every four words, or being politely let off entirely. The language exchange guide goes into the rest.

Countries, not just languages

A language spans many countries and they do not sound alike. The countries hub lists which countries speak what, when each is online, and how the accents differ - useful once you are past the beginner stage and want deliberate variety.

Frequently asked questions

Is language practice on TalkLive free?

Yes - unlimited voice and text practice at no cost, no card and no trial period. Country filters, which is how you target a language, are free too.

How do I find a native speaker of a specific language?

Filter to the countries where it is spoken. Each language page lists them with local peak hours, which matters - a country in the middle of its night has almost nobody online.

What if I am a complete beginner?

Start anyway. Say you are learning, keep the first calls to a few minutes, and let the other person carry more of it. Waiting until you feel ready is the most common reason people never start speaking at all.

Will people correct my mistakes?

If you ask them to. Say what you want at the start - most people default to being polite and letting errors go, which is comfortable and useless.

Is this a replacement for lessons?

No, a complement. Lessons give structure and correction; strangers give volume and unpredictability. Learners who do both progress noticeably faster than those who do either alone.
