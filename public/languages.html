How to use TalkLive for language practice

Make your goal clear, keep it mutual and treat every match as a person rather than a free lesson.

Choose the interface

Open the localized page that makes controls and safety information easiest for you to understand.

Choose voice or text

Use voice for spontaneous speech and listening, or text for written practice and quieter situations.

Confirm the language

Ask which language both people want to use and whether either person wants corrections.

Practise, thank and move on

Focus on one useful goal, avoid turning the call into an interrogation, and end courteously.

Supported localized entry pages

TalkLive currently provides localized homepages for Español, Português, Français, Deutsch, Italiano, Русский, Türkçe and Bahasa Indonesia.

Additional entry pages are available in العربية, فارسی, हिन्दी, اردو, বাংলা, 中文, 日本語 and 한국어. The legal policies remain the source of truth if a translated summary and an English policy ever differ.

Interface language is not match language

Choosing a translated page changes how TalkLive is presented to you. It does not place you in a certified language classroom or guarantee that the next person speaks that language. Country preferences are also not language filters: countries are multilingual, location labels are not identity proof, and a preferred-country match may not be available.

Start each session with a simple agreement: which language, how long, and whether corrections are welcome. If the match is not suitable, use Next without blaming the other person; they joined for their own conversation goal too.

Build a repeatable practice habit

For English, begin with Practice English Speaking and the 30-day speaking routine. The broader Language Exchange guide explains how to make the practice mutual rather than treating a stranger as an unpaid tutor.

A useful session can be five minutes: introduce yourself without identifying details, ask two open questions, notice one phrase you want to reuse, and finish with a clear thank-you. Consistency matters more than forcing every random match into a long lesson.

Practise safely across languages

Misunderstandings happen more easily across language levels, so clarify rather than escalating. At the same time, language difficulty is not a reason to ignore pressure for money, private photos, verification codes, exact location or off-platform contact. The Safety Center applies in every language.

Translation feedback is welcome through Contact. Include the page, the current wording, the suggested wording and the dialect or regional context so a correction can be reviewed accurately.

Frequently asked questions

Does TalkLive match me by language?

Not as a certified language-matching service. You can use translated interfaces and optional preferences, but you should confirm the conversation language with each participant.

Does choosing a country guarantee a native speaker?

No. A country preference neither guarantees a match nor proves nationality, residence, language or fluency.

Can I practise without speaking?

Yes. Text mode supports written practice without microphone permission. Voice mode is better suited to listening and spontaneous pronunciation practice.

Are people required to correct me?

No. Ask first. Random participants are not tutors, and correction should be mutual, limited and welcome.

Do I need to share my real name or social profile?

No. Language practice does not require identifying information. Keep exact location, school, workplace and private accounts to yourself.

What if a translation is unclear?

Use the Contact page to report the exact phrase and language. For privacy, billing and legal questions, check the current official policy pages.
