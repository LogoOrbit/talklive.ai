Random Call - a Free Voice Call With Someone New

A random call on TalkLive works like a phone call with one difference: you do not choose who answers. It runs in your browser over Wi-Fi or mobile data, so there is no phone number to share, no calling credit to spend and no app to install.

A phone call, minus the phone number

Everything you need to know about how a random call is placed, carried and ended.

No number on either side

The call is set up inside the browser. Neither person ever sees the other's phone number.

International at no cost

A call to the other side of the world costs the same as one across the street: nothing beyond your normal data.

Light on data

Audio-only calls use a small fraction of the data a video call needs, so they work on mobile data and weaker connections.

Encrypted in transit

Calls use WebRTC, which encrypts audio between the two browsers. TalkLive never records or stores it.

Hang up or redial

Next ends this call and starts searching for the next one. Hang Up ends calling altogether.

Call them back later

If you both add each other as friends, you can call back directly on another day without exchanging numbers.

How to make a random call

The first call takes one permission prompt. After that it is a single tap.

Tap to Talk

Press the button on the TalkLive homepage. Your browser asks once for microphone access.

Allow the microphone

Choose Allow. TalkLive only uses the microphone during a call.

Get connected

TalkLive finds another adult who is searching and connects the call - usually within seconds at busy times.

Talk, then Next or Hang Up

Stay as long as you like. Next finds a new person; Hang Up stops.

How a browser call actually reaches the other person

When both of you are matched, your browsers use WebRTC - the same open standard behind most web calling - to agree on the best route for the audio. Often that is a direct connection. On strict networks, such as many mobile carriers, offices and university Wi-Fi, a direct route is blocked, so the call is relayed through TalkLive's TURN server instead. Either way the audio is encrypted in transit, and neither route records it.

This is why a random call does not need a phone number, a SIM card or calling credit. It is ordinary internet traffic, which also means it works the same in every country where the site loads. The Journal explains the whole process step by step in what happens in the two seconds after you press Start.

How much data does a random call use?

Voice is compressed with the Opus codec, which is designed for speech and adapts to your connection. A typical call uses somewhere in the region of tens of megabytes per hour - a fraction of what a video call of the same length needs. If you are on a limited mobile plan, voice is the cheapest way to have a real-time conversation online, and text chat uses less still.

If the call will not connect or you cannot hear anything

No microphone prompt, or "permission denied": open your browser's site settings for talklive.app and set Microphone to Allow, then reload. On iPhone and iPad, also check Settings, then Safari (or your browser), then Microphone.

You can hear them but they cannot hear you: another app may be holding the microphone. Close other calling or recording apps, and check that the right input is selected if you use a headset.

Echo: use headphones. Echo almost always means the other person's voice is coming out of your speaker and back into your microphone.

Silence on both sides: tap Next. Very restrictive networks occasionally block even relayed calls; switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data usually fixes it.

Call etiquette for a call you did not plan

Random calls are spontaneous on both ends, so give the other person a second to settle - they may still be putting headphones in. Start with your greeting, and a quick "can you hear me okay?" saves a lot of confusion. Mute yourself if you need to step away, rather than leaving someone listening to a television.

Nervous about calls in general? That is common, and it gets easier with practice. Why your heart races when the phone rings is a gentle place to start.

Frequently asked questions

Is a random call free?

Yes. Calls are free on TalkLive and use your internet connection, so there are no calling charges, international or otherwise. Normal mobile data rates apply if you are not on Wi-Fi.

Will the other person get my phone number?

No. Calls run inside the browser over WebRTC. No phone number is involved on either side.

Do I need to install an app?

No. TalkLive works in any modern browser on Android, iPhone, Windows, Mac and Linux. You can add it to your home screen if you want an app-like icon.

Are random calls recorded?

TalkLive does not record or store call audio. The other participant could record on their own device, so avoid sharing sensitive information.

Can I choose who I call?

Not for a random call - that is the point. You can set preferences such as country, and you can call friends you have added directly.
