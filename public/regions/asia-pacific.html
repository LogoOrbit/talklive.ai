Random chat across East Asia, Southeast Asia & Oceania

This is the most linguistically varied region on TalkLive and the one where language practice drives the most conversations. Japanese and Korean are among the most-requested languages anywhere - far more learners want to practise them than native speakers show up - while Filipino, Malaysian and Singaporean users are often fluent English speakers whom learners elsewhere actively look for.

Time zones spread widely. Bangkok, Jakarta and Ho Chi Minh City sit at UTC+7; Manila, Kuala Lumpur, Singapore and Shanghai at UTC+8; Tokyo and Seoul at UTC+9; Sydney at UTC+10 or +11; and Auckland at UTC+12 or +13. The practical effect is that the region rolls online from east to west through the evening: New Zealand first, then Australia, then Japan and Korea, then Southeast Asia.

Voice suits the region for a familiar reason: typing Japanese, Korean, Thai or Vietnamese on a phone means switching input modes, and spoken conversation avoids that. Many users here are also noticeably more comfortable with voice than with video, which is the format TalkLive offers.

Japan

Japanese is one of the most-requested and least-supplied languages on any language-exchange platform - demand from learners vastly outstrips the number of native speakers who show up. Japanese users who do come are typically here to practise English and are noticeably more comfortable with voice than video, which is exactly the format on offer.

Languages you will hear: Japanese (こんにちは). Local time: UTC+9 (JST), busiest around 10pm - 1am JST. Common topics: travel, games, anime and music, work culture and practising English.

Around Japan: Tokyo - the largest metro area on earth and the most active Japanese chat city; Osaka - a dialect and a comic sensibility that other Japanese speakers immediately recognise.

South Korea

South Korea has among the fastest internet and the longest study hours in the world, and both show: chat traffic is late, concentrated, and heavily weighted toward English practice. Korean users tend to open cautiously and warm up fast, and voice-only removes the appearance pressure that makes video-first platforms a hard sell here.

Languages you will hear: Korean (안녕하세요). Local time: UTC+9 (KST), busiest around 10pm - 2am KST. Common topics: music and film, study and exams, travel and language exchange in both directions.

Around South Korea: Seoul - roughly half the country lives in its metro area, so "Korean chat" is largely Seoul chat; Busan - a coastal accent Koreans can place instantly.

China

Mandarin has more first-language speakers than any other language on earth, and demand from learners is enormous - a Mandarin match is one of the most valuable things a language learner can get. Chinese users on international platforms are typically here specifically for English practice and arrive better prepared than almost anyone, often with topics ready.

Languages you will hear: Mandarin (你好) and Cantonese (你好 (néih hóu)). Local time: UTC+8 (CST), busiest around 9pm - 1am CST. Common topics: study and work, travel, food and trading language practice both ways.

Around China: Shanghai - the most internationally connected matches in the country; Beijing - the standard Mandarin accent that learners are taught.

Indonesia

Indonesia is one of the most mobile-first countries in the world - for a large share of users the phone is the only computer they have ever owned, which is why a browser-based chat with nothing to install matters more here than almost anywhere. Bahasa Indonesia is deliberately easy to learn and widely shared across a thousand islands, so strangers from opposite ends of the country still match cleanly.

Languages you will hear: Indonesian (Halo, apa kabar?) and Javanese (Sugeng enjing). Local time: UTC+7 to +9 (WIB/WITA/WIT), busiest around 8pm - 1am WIB. Common topics: football and badminton, music, studying abroad and religion and family, handled respectfully.

Around Indonesia: Jakarta - traffic so bad that a long commute call is a genuine use case here; Surabaya - a port city with a blunter, faster conversational style than the capital; Bandung - a university city, so the median match is a student.

Philippines

The Philippines has one of the highest rates of comfortable, conversational English in Asia, which makes Filipino users some of the most sought-after language-practice partners on the platform - and unusually patient ones. A very large night-shift workforce serving American business hours also means the country has real chat traffic at 4am local time, when almost everywhere else is silent.

Languages you will hear: Filipino (Kumusta?), English (Hello) and Cebuano (Kumusta ka?). Local time: UTC+8 (PHT), busiest around 9pm - 2am PHT. Common topics: basketball, karaoke and music, working abroad and family.

Around the Philippines: Manila - a huge night-shift workforce, so its "evening" traffic runs at odd hours by local clock; Cebu City - Cebuano rather than Tagalog is the first language for most matches here; Davao - a slower, friendlier pace that regulars notice immediately.

Vietnam

Vietnam has one of the fastest-growing English-learning populations in the world and a young, phone-first internet culture, which together make it one of the quickest-rising sources of traffic on the platform. Vietnamese is tonal, so voice practice is worth far more here than text ever could be.

Languages you will hear: Vietnamese (Xin chào). Local time: UTC+7 (ICT), busiest around 9pm - 1am ICT. Common topics: study and career, food, football and practising English out loud.

Around Vietnam: Ho Chi Minh City - the country's commercial engine and its busiest source of English learners; Hanoi - the northern accent, which is the one taught to learners.

Thailand

Thailand combines a large domestic Thai-speaking user base with an unusually large population of foreigners living there long-term, so matches inside the country split between Thai and English more evenly than in most of Southeast Asia. Thai is tonal and notoriously hard to type, which pushes people toward voice.

Languages you will hear: Thai (สวัสดี). Local time: UTC+7 (ICT), busiest around 9pm - 1am ICT. Common topics: food and travel, music, work and visas and language practice.

Around Thailand: Bangkok - a huge visiting and expatriate population, so English matches inside Thailand are common; Chiang Mai - a remote-work hub, which gives it real weekday-daytime chat traffic.

Malaysia

Malaysia is one of the few countries where a single person will comfortably switch between Malay, English, Mandarin and Tamil in one conversation, and Malaysian English has enough of its own grammar to be its own thing. That makes Malaysian matches unusually adaptable - they will find a shared language with almost anyone.

Languages you will hear: Malay (Apa khabar?), English (Hello), Mandarin (你好) and Tamil (Vanakkam). Local time: UTC+8 (MYT), busiest around 9pm - 1am MYT. Common topics: food, above all else, football and badminton, work and study and travel around the region.

Around Malaysia: Kuala Lumpur - four languages in routine daily use, and matches here reflect all four; Penang - a smaller, food-obsessed city with a distinct Hokkien-inflected English.

Singapore

Singapore is small, dense and four-language official, which produces some of the most efficiently multilingual matches on the platform. Its position also makes it a natural crossroads: a Singaporean evening reaches India, China, Australia and Southeast Asia simultaneously.

Languages you will hear: English (Hello lah), Mandarin (你好), Malay (Apa khabar?) and Tamil (Vanakkam). Local time: UTC+8 (SGT), busiest around 9pm - 1am SGT. Common topics: food, work and study, travel and the region's politics, carefully.

Around Singapore: Singapore - a city and a country at once, with four official languages inside it.

Australia

Australia's evening lands in the middle of the Asian night and the European morning, which makes it one of the best-placed countries for genuinely cross-continental matches. It is also a heavily migrant country, so an Australian match is very often a first-generation one and the conversation goes somewhere unexpected.

Languages you will hear: English (G'day). Local time: UTC+8 to +11 (AWST/ACST/AEDT), busiest around 8pm - midnight AEST. Common topics: sport of every kind, travel, work and moving countries and weather, but hot.

Around Australia: Sydney - the country's busiest chat city and its most internationally mixed; Melbourne - a large student population that visibly changes the traffic in term time; Perth - three hours behind the east coast, which quietly extends the Australian evening.

New Zealand

New Zealand is the first country in the world into a new day, which gives it a genuinely odd position on a global chat platform: a New Zealand evening is an American morning and a European night. Kiwis are used to being far from everywhere, and a good share of matches here are explicitly about somewhere else.

Languages you will hear: English (Kia ora) and Māori (Kia ora). Local time: UTC+12 / UTC+13 (NZST/NZDT), busiest around 8pm - midnight NZST. Common topics: rugby, the outdoors, travel and being mistaken for Australian.

Around New Zealand: Auckland - the largest Polynesian city in the world, which shapes who you meet in it; Wellington - small, creative and disproportionately talkative.

What country preferences can and cannot do

TalkLive estimates each caller's country from their network connection and uses your preferences to favour matches from the countries you choose. It does not match by city, does not verify anyone's location, and cannot promise a match from a given country at a given moment - the live queue is whoever happens to be online. A VPN will also change the country you appear to be in.

Whoever you meet, the same rules apply everywhere: TalkLive is for adults only, you can leave any call instantly, and you can block or report anyone who breaks the community guidelines. See the safety centre for what TalkLive protects and what it cannot.

Frequently asked questions

Can I practise Japanese or Korean here?

Yes, but expect more learners than native speakers. Set a country preference for Japan or South Korea, be patient, and offer something in return - most Japanese and Korean users are here to practise English.

Is TalkLive available in China?

TalkLive is a website and works wherever it can be reached. Some networks in mainland China restrict foreign services, so availability there is not guaranteed.

When is the best time to find someone from Australia?

Australian evenings, roughly 8pm to midnight in Sydney, which is mid-morning in the UK and the previous evening on the US west coast.

Can I filter by city, such as Tokyo or Manila?

No. Matching uses country preferences only, and no location is verified. City notes on this page are background, not a filter.

Which languages does the app interface support in this region?

The interface is translated into Japanese, Korean, Chinese and Indonesian, among others. Calls themselves can be in any language you share with the other person.
