After the last train

Most Tokyo lines stop running a little after midnight, and that is exactly when TalkLive gets busy. Our peak, 15:00 to 21:00 UTC, is midnight to 6 am in Japan - the hours of convenience-store runs, late shifts and sleepless scrolling. If you are up then, the queue is at its fullest.

00:00-06:00TalkLive's busiest hours on Japan Standard Time (UTC+9, no daylight saving). The board above shows the local time where most of those people are.

Not a night owl? A Japanese evening, 7 to 11 pm, is the afternoon in India and Pakistan and late morning in Europe, so there is always someone starting their day as you end yours.

Japanese, English, or both

Japan has about 124 million people, and almost all of them speak Japanese. Many studied English for years at school but rarely get to speak it, which makes a low-pressure call with a stranger ideal practice: nobody knows who you are, and if a call goes badly you simply move on. If you are learning Japanese, people in Japan are usually patient and delighted that you are trying.

よろしくYoroshikuNice to meet you (and please be kind to me)
なるほどNaruhodoI see - the most useful word in a conversation
マジで？Maji de?Seriously?
おつかれOtsukareGood work today - a greeting at day's end
相槌 - aizuchi. Japanese listeners keep up a steady stream of small replies while the other person talks: はい (hai), ええ (ee), そうですね (sou desu ne). On a voice call they matter even more, because there is no nod to see. Silence from you can sound like you are not listening, so a quick "hai" or "naruhodo" goes a long way.

Prefer to read the interface in Japanese? TalkLive in Japanese has the whole app translated, and our languages feature covers practising Japanese with native speakers.

What to talk about

The safe openers work everywhere: where you are, what time it is there, what you ate. Then the things Japan loves to talk about - the seasons (cherry blossom in spring, autumn leaves in November), ramen and convenience-store food, baseball and Shohei Ohtani, anime, games and J-pop, and the trains, which visitors never stop being amazed by. Ask about someone's hometown: regional pride, from Osaka's humour to Hokkaido's winters, is a great way in.

Privacy and safety

Keep your full name, address, school or workplace and social accounts to yourself in a first conversation. Moving quickly to LINE is the first step of most romance and investment scams in Japan, so be wary of anyone who asks for your LINE ID within minutes, talks about money or crypto, or asks for photos. Use Add friend inside TalkLive if you want to talk again - nobody sees your number or ID. Our guide to chat scams has more. Every call and chat has Report and Block, and TalkLive is for adults 18 and over only.

Need to talk? Yorisoi Hotline answers free on 0120-279-338 at any hour, and TELL Lifeline offers support in English on 03-5774-0992. In an emergency in Japan, call 110 (police) or 119 (ambulance).

Questions people ask - よくある質問

Can I talk in Japanese on TalkLive?

Yes. Speak whichever language you and your match share. The whole interface is available in Japanese at talklive.app/ja/, and choosing it makes you more likely to meet other people who use TalkLive in Japanese.

Is TalkLive free in Japan?

Yes. Voice and text chat are free, need no account, no app and no phone number, and preferring Japan in the country filter is free.

When is Japan busiest on TalkLive?

TalkLive is busiest worldwide between 15:00 and 21:00 UTC, which is midnight to 6 am in Japan (JST, UTC+9, all year). Japanese evenings, 7 pm to 11 pm, line up with the afternoon in India and late morning in Europe.

Can I use TalkLive to practise English?

Yes. Pick "English practice" as an interest and say in your first sentence that you are practising. Many people on TalkLive speak English as a second language too, so mistakes are normal and nobody minds.

Does anyone see my phone number, LINE ID or face?

No. Calls run in the browser, no number or ID is exchanged and there is no video. Use Add friend inside TalkLive instead of sharing your LINE ID.

Is there someone to talk to if I am in crisis in Japan?

Yes. Yorisoi Hotline answers free on 0120-279-338 at any hour, and TELL Lifeline offers support in English on 03-5774-0992. In an emergency, call 110 for the police or 119 for an ambulance.
