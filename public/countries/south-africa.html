A country of many voices

South Africa's 2022 census counted about 62 million people. isiZulu is the most widely spoken home language, followed by isiXhosa and Afrikaans, and English is the language of business, government and most conversations between strangers. Nelson Mandela's line - talk to a man in a language he understands and it goes to his head; talk to him in his language and it goes to his heart - is quoted here for a reason.

Ubuntu, the idea that a person is a person through other people, is a word South Africans actually use, and it shows in how readily they talk to someone new. Expect warmth, a sense of humour that runs dry, and a lot of questions about where you are calling from.

Speak South African

South African English borrows freely from Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa and everywhere else, and it has a vocabulary all its own. Use a few of these and your match will notice.

What to talk about

Rugby is a national conversation - the Springboks won the Rugby World Cup in 2019 and again in 2023 - and so are cricket and football. The braai is a ritual and an argument (gas or wood? who is in charge of the fire?). Music runs from amapiano, which went from Pretoria and Johannesburg townships to the world, to gqom, kwaito and jazz. Load-shedding, taxis, Joburg versus Cape Town, the best bunny chow in Durban, and what "just now" actually means will keep a call going for as long as you want.

Privacy and safety

Keep your full name, ID number, address, workplace and social media to yourself in a first conversation. Never share a banking OTP or PIN, and be careful with anyone who asks for money, airtime, vouchers or crypto, asks for photos, or wants to move to WhatsApp straight away.
