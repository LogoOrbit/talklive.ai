Tea first, questions later

Hospitality in Afghanistan is not a courtesy, it is a rule: a guest is given tea and the best seat before anyone asks why they came. That carries over to conversation. Greetings are long and sincere, people ask after your family and your health, and they mean it.

Millions of Afghans live abroad, in Iran, Pakistan, Germany, Turkey, the United States and elsewhere, so the voice that answers may be thousands of kilometres from Kabul and happy to speak Dari or Pashto for an evening.

How to say hello

Salaam alaikumWa alaikum salaamThe greeting everyone uses, in every language of the country.
Chetor asti?Khob astam, tashakorDari: "How are you?" - "I am well, thank you."
Sanga yee?Kha yam, mananaPashto: "How are you?" - "I am well, thank you."
Khosh amadidZenda bashi"Welcome" - "May you live long", the warm reply to almost anything.

Tashakor (Dari) and manana (Pashto) both mean thank you. Jan after a name means dear, and is used for friends of any age. Inshallah closes most plans.

Come, come, whoever you are.A line long attributed to Rumi, who was born in Balkh in 1207. Poetry is still quoted in ordinary conversation here, and a verse is a welcome answer.

What to talk about

Cricket above all: the national team and its spinners are a source of real joy, and everyone has an opinion. Kabuli pulao and who makes it properly, mantu, bolani and green tea with cardamom. Nowruz and the red tulips of Mazar-i-Sharif. Kite flying, the attan, music old and new, and the mountains. Ask about someone's home province and listen.

Many people here are practising English for study or work. If that is you, add the English practice interest before you start.

Stay safe

Share only what you are comfortable sharing. Keep your full name, exact location, workplace and social accounts to yourself, and never send money, codes or photos to someone you have just met. End the call with anyone who pressures you or asks to move to another app straight away. Our scam guide lists the usual tricks, and every call and chat has Report and Block.

Questions people ask

Is TalkLive free in Afghanistan?

Yes. Voice calls and text chat are free, with no account, no phone number and no camera. Choosing Afghanistan as a preferred country is free as well.

Can I talk in Dari or Pashto?

Yes. Speak any language you and your match share. People chat in Dari, Pashto, Uzbek and English, and many Afghans abroad also speak Urdu, German or Turkish.

Does it work on a slow connection?

A voice call needs far less data than video, about 0.3 to 0.5 MB a minute, and text chat works on almost any connection.

When are people from Afghanistan online?

TalkLive is busiest between 15:00 and 21:00 UTC, which is 7:30 in the evening to 1:30 at night in Kabul (UTC+4:30).

Is the call private?

Calls go directly between the two devices and are never recorded. You use a temporary name, and you choose what to share about yourself.
