When to find people from Pakistan

TalkLive is busiest between 15:00 and 21:00 UTC. In Pakistan Standard Time (UTC+5, no daylight saving) that is 8 pm to 2 am PKT, and Pakistani users keep the queue lively well past that - late night is genuinely late here. The quietest stretch is 5 to 10 am PKT. Measured from TalkLive's own hourly match counts in late September and early October 2026.

For overseas Pakistanis: 10 pm in Karachi or Lahore is 6 pm in the UK in summer (5 pm in winter), 9 pm in Dubai and around 1 pm on the US East Coast. That overlap is why so many matches here are between people at home and family-age Pakistanis abroad.

Urdu, Punjabi and everything in between

Urdu is the national language and the one most matches settle into, even though it is the mother tongue of a minority of Pakistanis. Punjabi is the most widely spoken first language, and Pashto, Sindhi, Saraiki and Balochi are all common; English is an official language and turns up in a large share of conversations, especially with students. Switching between them mid-sentence is normal, and nobody expects you to stick to one.

Hindi speakers from India understand spoken Urdu with little difficulty, and vice versa, which is why cross-border matches so often work smoothly by voice even though the two languages look nothing alike in writing. Our feature Why South Asia Talks After Midnight explores that shared spoken language.

What people talk about

Cricket comes first, as it always has. After that: university admissions and entry tests, freelancing and remote work for overseas clients, family, and food - endlessly, and with strong regional loyalties between Karachi, Lahore and Peshawar. Music and dramas are a reliable bridge with people from other countries, many of whom know Pakistani songs or serials without realising where they came from.

If you are practising English, Pakistan is a good place to look: plenty of people here want speaking practice too, so you can trade - ten minutes in English, ten in Urdu. Our English speaking practice guide explains how to make that work.

Staying safe

Never share your CNIC number, a bank or mobile-wallet PIN, or a one-time code with anyone you meet in a chat, whatever reason they give. Prize and lottery messages, "verification" requests and job offers that need an upfront fee are the most common scripts. Be just as careful with anyone who asks for photos or pushes to move to WhatsApp straight away. Our field guide to chat scams lists the patterns.

If anyone is abusive, tap Report or Block - blocked people are not matched with you again. Online harassment and fraud can be reported to the FIA's cybercrime wing, now part of the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a free Pakistani chat site with voice calls?

Yes. TalkLive is free for voice and text, needs no account, and lets you prefer Pakistan in the country filter.

Yes. The full interface is available in Urdu, right to left, at talklive.app/ur/.

Do I have to speak Urdu?

No. Many matches are in English or Punjabi, and switching languages mid-conversation is completely normal.

Not always - it depends on who is searching at that moment. Evenings after 8 pm PKT are the busiest time.

No. Calls run in the browser over the internet, so no phone number is exchanged.
