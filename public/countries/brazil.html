Talk to strangers in Brazil

In Brazilian Portuguese a chat is a bate-papo, and Brazil is a country that loves one: with neighbours, with taxi drivers, with whoever is next to you in the queue. Start one by voice or text, free, with no account, no number and no camera.

The biggest conversation in Latin America

Brazil is home to about 203 million people, counted in the 2022 census, and it is the largest country in South America by a wide margin. It is the only Portuguese-speaking country in the Americas, which makes it the biggest Portuguese-speaking nation on earth. It spans four time zones, though most Brazilians live on Brasília time, three hours behind UTC.

Brazilians are, by reputation and in practice, among the most sociable people anywhere. Messages tend to be voice notes, conversations tend to run long, and a stranger who asks a friendly question usually gets a warm answer. If you are lonely, bored or simply curious, there are few better places to start.

If you are learning Portuguese, Brazilian Portuguese is a generous language to practise by voice: open vowels, music in every sentence, and speakers who are happy to slow down. Say you are learning in your first message. Our guide to language exchange explains how to make it a fair swap.

What to talk about

Football is close to a shared religion: Brazil has won the men's World Cup five times, more than any other country, and everyone has a club - Flamengo, Corinthians, Palmeiras, São Paulo and dozens more - and an opinion on the national team. Music is next: samba, bossa nova, MPB, sertanejo and funk carioca each have their own world. Ask about Carnival, the best açaí, the novela everyone is watching, the beach versus the countryside, or what someone means by saudade - the word that famously has no exact translation.

Privacy and safety

Keep your full name, address, CPF, workplace and social media to yourself in a first conversation. Never share a bank code, a Pix key you did not mean to give out, or a one-time password, and be careful with anyone who asks for money, gift cards or crypto, asks for photos, or pushes to move to WhatsApp straight away - our guide to chat scams describes the usual scripts. Every call and chat has Report and Block, and TalkLive is for adults 18 and over only.

Need to talk? CVV answers on 188, free and at any hour. In an emergency in Brazil, call SAMU on 192 or the police on 190.

Perguntas - questions people ask

Can I chat in Portuguese on TalkLive?

Sim. Speak whichever language you and your match share, and switch the whole interface to Portuguese at talklive.app/pt/ if you prefer.

Is TalkLive free in Brazil?

Yes. Voice and text chat are free, need no account, and preferring Brazil in the country filter is free.

When is Brazil busiest on TalkLive?

TalkLive is busiest worldwide between 15:00 and 21:00 UTC - noon to 6 pm in Brasília time, which Brazil keeps all year since it dropped daylight saving in 2019. Brazilian evenings are quieter but often match you with Asia's morning.

Will I always be matched with someone in Brazil?

Not always. A country preference steers matching, but it depends on who is searching at that moment. Adding Portugal as a second preferred country keeps the conversation in Portuguese more often.

Does anyone see my phone number or face?

No. Calls run in the browser, no number is exchanged and there is no video.

Is there someone to talk to if I am in crisis in Brazil?

Yes. CVV, the Centro de Valorização da Vida, answers on 188, free and at any hour. In an emergency call SAMU on 192 or the police on 190.

Shall we have a chat? Someone in São Paulo, Rio or Recife is up for one.
