# TalkLive — 30-day plan (22 Sep – 21 Oct 2026)

Advisory write-up based on the 30-day data to 21 Sep 2026, cross-checked against
the code in this repo. Companion to [GROWTH.md](../GROWTH.md) (strategy) and
[docs/action-plan.md](action-plan.md) (ads tuning); this document is narrower —
what to do in the next four weeks, in order.

**Not legal advice.** §5 flags questions for a qualified lawyer; it does not
answer them.

---

## 0. The diagnosis in one paragraph

Traffic is not the constraint. 850 visits/day producing a peak of 10–24
concurrent sockets means the matchable pool is near-empty for most of the day,
and `server/index.js` then splits that pool in two by hard-gating on `mode`
(voice vs text, line 2169). A 1-in-8 match rate is not a matching-algorithm
problem — it is an arithmetic problem: most searches have literally nobody on
the other side. Every other number in the report is downstream of that. Signups,
reports, ad revenue and pricing all move when a user has one good conversation,
and none of them move much until then. **The whole of weeks 1–2 should go to
concurrency; do not buy traffic this month.** Adding visitors to a product that
cannot match them burns money and raises the report rate.

Little's Law makes the size of the hole concrete. If 850 daily sessions each
spend ~3 minutes matchable, average concurrent matchable users is
`850 × 3 / 1440 ≈ 1.8`. The observed 10–24 peak is total connected sockets
(`io.engine.clientsCount`, line 1923), which includes people reading landing
pages who never enter a queue. **Real matchable concurrency at peak is probably
single digits, and near zero off-peak.** Confirm this before anything else — it
is metric M1 in §6 and it changes how ambitious the rest of the plan should be.

There are only four ways out, and you should run three of them:

| Lever | Meaning | Doing it? |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrate **space** | Fewer pools, fewer filters, one queue | Yes — P0.1, P0.3 |
| Concentrate **time** | Scheduled rush hours instead of 24/7 thinness | Yes — P0.2 |
| Go **async** | A wait always ends in something, not a spinner | Partly — P0.4 |
| **Fake** supply | Bots, inflated counters, fake "1,240 online" | **No.** See §4 |

## 1. Prioritised plan

Impact is rated against the constraint above (match rate and conversations per
visitor), not against revenue. Effort is engineering days for one developer.

### P0 — Liquidity (week 1–2)

| # | Item | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0.1 | Merge voice and text into one queue | **Very high** | M — 3–4d |
| P0.2 | Rush hours + return-to-site notifications | **High** | L — 1–2d |
| P0.3 | Full wait-fallback ladder (replaces the 10s flag) | **High** | M — 2–3d |
| P0.4 | Honest activity counters | Medium-high | L — 1d |
| P0.5 | Instrument the funnel (§6 M1–M6) | **High** (enables all else) | M — 2–3d |

**P0.1 — one queue.** Today `mutuallyCompatible()` returns false whenever
`seeker.mode !== candidate.mode`. The comment's reasoning is right — a mic-less
chatter must never be dropped into a call — but the conclusion is too strong.
Make **text the universal floor and voice an in-call upgrade**: everyone matches
in a single pool; the session opens in text; if both sides have a mic and both
tap "turn on voice", it escalates to WebRTC in place. A voice-seeker who lands
with a text-only partner gets a real conversation instead of a spinner, and the
safety property the comment protects is preserved because nobody is ever placed
into audio without an explicit tap. Expect this alone to roughly double
effective pool depth at peak. Keep a "voice only" preference for users who
genuinely want nothing else — but make it a *preference the fallback relaxes*,
not a hard gate.

**P0.2 — rush hours.** At this volume you cannot be liquid 24/7, so stop trying.
Pick 2–3 daily windows aligned to the evening hours of the converting markets —
roughly **15:00–19:00 UTC** covers Nigeria evening through Pakistan late evening
in one block, with a second window for Türkiye/Iran. Publish them on the site,
show a countdown when the site is quiet, and email/push the 218 existing accounts
before each window. Concentrating the same 850 visits into narrower windows is
the single cheapest multiplier on concurrency you have, and it needs no
matchmaking changes. The push and mailer plumbing already exists
(`server/push.js`, `server/mailer.js`).

**P0.3 — fallback ladder.** `RANDOM_FALLBACK_MS = 10000` currently flips one
boolean that drops every preference filter at once. Replace it with a staged
ladder, and never let a user watch an empty spinner past ~45 seconds:

| Elapsed | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| 0–10s | Strict: all preferences honoured |
| 10–25s | Drop gender and country preferences (today's behaviour) |
| 25–45s | Drop mode: match cross-pool into text (needs P0.1) |
| 45s+ | Offer an exit that is not failure — see below |

At 45s, offer, in order of what is available: reconnect with a recent partner,
a small moderated group text room, or "notify me when someone joins" (push or
email, honest wording, one tap). **Tune these thresholds from the patience curve
(M4), not from these guesses** — the numbers above are a starting point.

**P0.4 — honest counters.** The current global counter is honest in the sense
that it reports real sockets, but it is not *useful*: it counts landing-page
readers as "online", which makes an empty queue look busy and erodes trust the
first time a user waits two minutes next to a "17 online" badge. Show two
numbers instead:

- **"N waiting to talk right now"** — queue depth, which is the number that
  actually predicts a match.
- **"N people talked in the last hour"** — almost never zero, honest, and it
  tells a user arriving at a dead hour that the site is alive.

When the queue is empty, say so and offer the 45s options immediately rather
than starting a doomed search.

**P0.5 — instrumentation.** Ship M1–M6 from §6 before the A/B test in week 2,
or you will not be able to read its result.

### P1 — Ads (week 2–3)

| # | Item | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1.1 | Block forex / fake-alert / clickbait creatives by category | **High** | L — 0.5d |
| P1.2 | Zero ad units on queue and call surfaces | **High** | L — config |
| P1.3 | Clean-ads A/B test, 14 days | High (decision value) | M — 2d |

Four units per screen is not primarily a revenue-vs-UX trade here. In a
two-sided realtime market, a user who clicks a fake-alert banner during a 30s
wait costs you **two** matches — theirs and the one their absence denies someone
else. The cost of an ad click-out is superlinear in a thin queue, which is
exactly the regime you are in. Fake-alert and clickbait creatives additionally
poison the trust you need for the safety story in §5 and for any paid tier.

The mechanics are already built: `public/ads-config.json` supports
`maxSlotsPerPage` and a per-format kill switch, and `server/index.js` merges the
`ADS_CONFIG` env var over it, so a variant is a Fly secret flip rather than a
deploy. `pauseDuringCall` already protects live calls — extend the same
protection to the *searching* state, which today is deliberately excluded and is
precisely the moment a click-out is most expensive.

**Test design:**

- **Control:** current config (4 units, all categories).
- **Treatment:** 0 units on queue/call surfaces, max 2 on content pages, forex
  and alert-style creatives blocked.
- Bucket on a stable anonymous client id, 50/50, 14 days.
- **Primary metric:** conversations >3 min per 100 visitors. **Secondary:**
  revenue per visitor-day. *Not* RPM — RPM rises when you show ads to fewer,
  more engaged people and tells you nothing about total revenue.

**Pre-commit the decision rule now, before you see data:** ship the treatment
unless revenue per visitor-day drops more than 25%. At ~12k visitors per arm you
can detect roughly a 15% relative change in match rate but *not* a small revenue
difference — so the test is powered to answer the liquidity question and not the
revenue question, and the decision rule has to reflect that asymmetry. Assumption
8 in §7 matters here: if current ad revenue is only a few dollars a day, this
trade is close to free.

### P2 — Pricing (week 3–4)

| # | Item | Impact | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| P2.1 | Fix the contradictions (copy only) | High (risk removal) | L — 1d |
| P2.2 | One Premium product, one price, 3 PPP tiers | High | M — 2–3d |
| P2.3 | Define or delete coins | Medium | L — 1d |
| P2.4 | One non-Stripe local rail | Medium-high | H — 4–6d |

**P2.1 — stop the bleeding first, it costs a day.** "$0 forever" contradicting
the Terms, inconsistent free-tier limits, a Premium tier with no price, and a
"Most popular" label on a tier that is not, are all fixable with copy edits and
all carry consumer-protection exposure (§5). Do these in week 3 regardless of
what happens with P2.2.

**P2.2 — regional pricing.** Stripe-only checkout at $4.99–$99.99 is priced for
the markets that convert *worst*. The US sends traffic at 1.5 accounts per 1,000
visits; PK/BD/NG/TR/IR convert best and are the markets least able to pay a
US-dollar list price. Collapse the six coin tiers into **one Premium
subscription at three PPP tiers**, assigned by IP country:

| Tier | Markets | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| A | US, UK, EU, CA, AU | $4.99 |
| B | TR, BR, MX, RU, MY, ZA | $2.49 |
| C | PK, BD, NG, IN, ID, EG, PH | $0.99–1.49 |

Annual at ~8× monthly. Expect VPN arbitrage into tier C — cap it by requiring
the billing country to match, accept some leakage, and note that leakage from a
market that currently pays $0 is not a loss. **Iran is a sanctions question, not
a pricing question — see §5.**

Sell one thing people already want and the code already has: preference filters
that *hold* (gender/country honoured without the fallback relaxing them),
priority position in the queue, and no ads. Do not sell anything that makes the
liquidity problem worse — a paid filter that further fragments a 10-person pool
is selling a worse product to your best users.

**P2.3 — coins.** Six tiers up to $99.99 for a token with no defined use is an
unbounded chargeback and consumer-protection liability. Either define exactly
what a coin buys (queue priority, filter-minutes, gifts) with a published
conversion rate, or remove the shop entirely. Removing it is the better
month-one choice: it is one page, and it takes the fake "Most popular" label
with it.

**P2.4 — payments.** Stripe does not meaningfully serve PK/BD/NG, which is where
your conversion is. Full local-rail integration (JazzCash, Easypaisa, bKash) is
more than a month of work, so scope week 4 to **one** rail with the best
effort-to-coverage ratio — **Paystack or Flutterwave for Nigeria** — and treat
it as a test of the thesis rather than a rollout. If NG conversion at $1.49 with
a local rail beats NG conversion at $4.99 on Stripe by a wide margin, the case
for the rest is made with data. A merchant-of-record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy)
is the alternative if you would rather buy coverage than build it; it costs more
per transaction and less in engineering and tax compliance.

### P3 — Safety and compliance (week 1, in parallel)

Not last because it is least important — §5 is a separate checklist because
parts of it are week-1 P0 risk items, not week-4 polish. 203 reports at ~2.6% of
matches, spiking to 51/day within a week of the text launch, is a real signal:
text makes abuse cheap, repeatable and persistent in a way voice does not.

## 2. Week-by-week

**Week 1 (22–28 Sep)** — P0.5 instrumentation, P0.4 honest counters, P0.2 rush
hours, and the §5 items marked *now*. Start P0.1.

**Week 2 (29 Sep – 5 Oct)** — Ship P0.1 one queue and P0.3 the ladder. Launch
the P1 A/B test once M1–M6 are reporting.

**Week 3 (6–12 Oct)** — P2.1 copy fixes and P2.3 coins. Read the A/B test at
day 10 but do not stop it early. Tune the ladder thresholds from M4.

**Week 4 (13–21 Oct)** — Call the A/B test and ship the winner. Ship P2.2 PPP
pricing. Start P2.4 on one rail. Re-read the funnel: match rate should be
materially up, and if it is not, the assumption set in §7 is where to look
first.

## 3. What not to do this month

- **Do not buy traffic.** The constraint is concurrency, not visitors.
- **Do not add filters or pools.** Every new dimension divides a pool of ~10.
- **Do not build video.** It is a different product with a harder safety profile
  and it does nothing for liquidity.
- **Do not chase the US and India.** 36% of traffic converting at 1.5/1,000 is a
  fit problem, not an optimisation problem. Serve the markets that already work.

## 4. On fake activity

The obvious fix for a thin queue is to inflate the counter, seed bot profiles,
or keep a user "searching" against nobody. Do not. It converts a fixable
liquidity problem into an unfixable trust problem, it is a deceptive-practices
exposure on top of the pricing issues in §5, and it destroys the one metric that
tells you whether any of this is working. The honest version — "3 waiting, 46
talked in the last hour", with a real fallback at 45 seconds — performs better
anyway, because a user who is told the truth once will come back at a rush hour.

Seeding real supply is legitimate: paid or incentivised hosts who are real
people and disclosed as such. That is a week-5+ project, not a month-one one.

## 5. Safety and compliance checklist

**This is a list of questions for a qualified lawyer, not answers.** Items
marked *now* are ones I would not wait on counsel to start, because they are
product decisions that reduce risk in any legal reading.

**Age assurance**
- A single 18+ checkbox is the weakest control available, and expectations for
  adult-oriented services have moved in several jurisdictions (UK Online Safety
  Act, various US state laws, EU DSA-adjacent obligations). **Ask counsel what
  applies given your actual traffic mix**, which is materially PK/BD/NG/TR/IR
  plus US/EU.
- *Now:* record a dated affirmation rather than a transient checkbox; re-affirm
  on first match; remove anything in marketing copy that reads as targeting
  minors or as adult content.

**The Online Now list**
- *Now:* exposing name + country + gender publicly is a targeting and stalking
  surface and a profiling question. Make it opt-in and off by default, coarsen
  country to region, and drop gender from the public list entirely.

**Voice-based gender estimation**
- This is the item I would take to counsel first. Inferring gender from voice is
  plausibly biometric-adjacent processing; under GDPR whether it touches Art. 9
  depends on use and is genuinely contested. **Get a specific opinion.**
- *Now, independent of the legal answer:* make it opt-in, let users state gender
  explicitly, never display an inference as fact, and do not gate a paid feature
  on it — the accuracy failures land on exactly the users least able to complain.

**Minor safety and abuse**
- A documented CSAM detection and reporting path, including the jurisdictions
  you must report to.
- Evidence retention for reports: what is kept, for how long, who can see it.
  `server/admin.js` has an audit log — verify a retention policy is actually
  defined rather than implied.
- A written takedown and escalation SLA, and ban-evasion handling (the same
  client id or IP reappearing after a ban).
- *Now:* given the text-launch report spike, add rate limits on first-contact
  messages and a block that persists across sessions.

**Consumer protection**
- *Now:* "$0 forever" contradicting the Terms, undefined coins sold up to
  $99.99, and a "Most popular" label on a tier that is not, are each a
  deceptive-practices question in multiple jurisdictions. P2.1 and P2.3 remove
  them in two days of copy work. Do it before adding a payment rail, not after.

**Jurisdiction**
- **Iran:** US sanctions make serving Iranian users a real question for any
  payment flow, and Stripe's terms answer it one way already. Iran is currently
  in your best-converting set, so this needs an actual opinion before P2.2 ships
  — not a guess.
- **EU:** if EU traffic is material, DSA notice-and-action and a named point of
  contact.
- Data residency and a DPA for whatever processes voice, if anything leaves the
  peer connection.

## 6. Metrics you are missing

The current reporting is volume and vanity — visitors, messages/day, total
reports. None of it can answer "is a user having a conversation, and do they
come back?" These are the gaps, roughly in order of value.

### Liquidity (M1–M6) — build these first

- **M1. Concurrent *matchable* users** — queue depth, p50/p95, by UTC hour and
  day of week. Not connected sockets. This is the capacity number the whole
  business runs on and it is currently not recorded; `recordPeakOnline()` stores
  the wrong quantity.
- **M2. Queue depth at the moment each search starts.** Lets you separate "no
  partner existed" from "a partner existed and the algorithm missed them" —
  two completely different bugs currently reported as one 1-in-8 number.
- **M3. Time-to-match distribution**, p50/p90. Never the mean; the mean of a
  wait distribution with a long tail is meaningless.
- **M4. The patience curve** — abandon-while-waiting rate as a function of
  elapsed seconds. This sets the P0.3 ladder thresholds empirically instead of
  by guess, and it is a half-day of instrumentation.
- **M5. The funnel, decomposed:** searches → matched → media connected →
  survived 30s → survived 3 min. "1 in 8 connections becomes a match" collapses
  at least three distinct stages; you cannot fix it until you know which one
  leaks.
- **M6. WebRTC/TURN failure rate by country.** Some meaningful share of "no
  match" in PK, IR and NG is likely NAT traversal failing, not an absent
  partner. `pairOnCooldown` already tracks pairs whose call never connected —
  aggregate it by country. This is cheap to measure and could be a large win;
  see [DEPLOY-TURN.md](../DEPLOY-TURN.md).

### Conversation quality (M7–M10)

- **M7. Median conversation duration**, and **% of matches over 3 minutes** —
  the "real conversation rate". `matchedAt` is already stamped on both profiles;
  this is mostly a surfacing job.
- **M8. Skip rate in the first 10 seconds**, per side. A high figure points at
  mismatch or a bad first impression; a high *one-sided* figure points at abuse.
- **M9. Messages per text match, and the one-sided ratio** — conversations where
  one party sends >80% of messages. A direct spam and harassment proxy, and the
  number that would have caught the mid-September report spike early.
- **M10. Heart / rematch rate.**

### Retention (M11–M14) — the biggest blind spot

- **M11. D1 / D7 / D30 return rate by anonymous client id**, not by account.
  Almost nobody signs up; account-based retention measures 218 people and misses
  25,000.
- **M12. Retention split by day-0 outcome** — users who had a >3 min
  conversation on their first visit versus users who did not. **This is the
  single most valuable number on this list.** It tells you the magnitude of the
  conversation→retention link and therefore whether the entire premise of this
  plan is right.
- **M13. Sessions per returning user per week.**
- **M14. Signups per *good conversation*** rather than per visit. The Sep 16
  release doubled signups; this metric says whether it did so by improving the
  product or by moving a button.

### Monetisation (M15–M18)

- **M15. Revenue per visitor-day, by country** — the denominator that makes the
  P1 test readable. RPM will mislead you.
- **M16. Ad click-out rate by surface** (landing / queue / call), and the
  estimated matches lost per click-out during a search.
- **M17. Checkout funnel by country:** pricing view → checkout start → payment
  success, plus **decline rate by country**. The decline rate is what proves or
  kills the Stripe-only thesis with evidence instead of argument.
- **M18. ARPU by PPP tier** after P2.2.

### Safety (M19–M22)

- **M19. Reports per 1,000 matches, split voice vs text.** Reports per *day* is
  confounded by volume; per-match normalisation is what tells you whether text is
  disproportionately abusive — which the Sep 7→14 sequence suggests it is.
- **M20. Report → action rate and median time to action.**
- **M21. Repeat-offender share and ban-evasion rate.**
- **M22. Share of first-time users whose *first* match was reported.** A bad
  first impression is a permanent loss, and this is the number that quantifies
  the retention cost of the report spike.

### Traffic quality (M23–M24)

- **M23. Bot/VPN share by country.** SG/CN/RU send 10.6% of visits and zero
  accounts. Instrument whether they open a socket and whether they ever enter a
  queue. If they never queue, they are bots and they are inflating the
  denominator of every conversion rate you have — which means your real US and
  India numbers are somewhat better than they look, and your real match rate is
  somewhat worse.
- **M24. Accounts and matches per 1,000 *engaged* visitors** (those who started a
  search), alongside the per-visit version. This is the conversion number to
  manage against.

## 7. Assumptions

Every one of these is falsifiable and several are load-bearing. If the plan
looks wrong, check here first.

1. **"~850 visitors/day"** means sessions or unique visitors from an analytics
   tool, not pageviews, and includes the bot/VPN share.
2. **"1 in 8 connections becomes a match"** means ~12.5% of *search attempts*
   result in a paired partner, and does **not** already net out WebRTC connect
   failures. If it does, M6 matters less and the pool is even thinner than I
   think.
3. **Peak concurrent 10–24** is total connected sockets — confirmed in
   `server/index.js:1923`, which emits `io.engine.clientsCount` — so it includes
   landing-page readers who never queue. Real matchable concurrency is materially
   lower. **This is the most load-bearing assumption in the document**; M1
   settles it in a day.
4. **Voice and text match in separate pools** — confirmed in
   `mutuallyCompatible()` at `server/index.js:2169`.
5. **Reports at 2.6% of matches** is reports ÷ matches over the same window, with
   matches counted per pairing rather than per participant.
6. **The Sep 16 signup doubling is causal to that release.** With 9→21/day and
   n=218 this could be partly noise or a traffic-mix shift. Treat it as a
   hypothesis; a holdout on the next release would settle it.
7. **A conversation drives retention.** M12 tests this directly. If day-0
   conversations do *not* predict return, the liquidity-first framing is wrong
   and the problem is positioning instead.
8. **Current ad revenue is small in absolute terms** — Adsterra-class RPMs on
   ~850 visits/day is plausibly low single-digit dollars per day — which makes
   trading ad density for liquidity nearly free. If actual revenue is materially
   higher, re-run the P1 decision rule with the real number.
9. **Moderation is one person plus the owner dashboard**, with no 24/7 coverage.
10. **No legal review has happened yet** on age assurance, the voice-based gender
    inference, or Iran.
11. **The product is adults-only by positioning but is not adult content**, and
    you intend to keep it that way. If that is wrong, §5 changes substantially.
12. **Engineering capacity is roughly one developer-month**, which is why P2.4
    is scoped to a single payment rail and video is not on the list.
